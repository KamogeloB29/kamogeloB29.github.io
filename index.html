University of Pretoria

Survival functions in grouped insurance data

Insurance lapse data is usually recorded in grouped time intervals rather than exact times, and many policies are still active when observation ends. The research asks whether the Lindley distribution is a useful alternative to the Exponential, Weibull and Lognormal models for describing how long policies stay active.

Exponential1 parameter. Constant hazard.
Lindley1 parameter. Hazard can rise, fall or stay constant, depending on the parameter.
Weibull2 parameters. The shape parameter sets a rising, falling or constant hazard.
Lognormal2 parameters. Suits skewed lifetimes. The hazard rises, then falls.
  1. Estimate parameters by maximum likelihood on grouped data. An event inside an interval contributes S(a) − S(b), and a right-censored policy contributes S(c).
  2. Compare the models with log-likelihood, AIC, BIC, goodness-of-fit measures and survival and hazard plots.
  3. Run a simulation study across sample sizes of 100, 250 and 500 and censoring levels of 10%, 20% and 30%, judged on bias and mean squared error.
  4. Apply the models to insurance policy lapse data from 2017 to 2019.
  5. Present the work in an interactive Shiny app with the theory, data application, simulation results and key findings.
